# Candidate Business Questions (Step 3)

Each question is derived from a specific pattern in `EDA_FINDINGS.md`.
Ratings: **Decision** = usefulness for a manager's decision (top priority);
**Novelty** = specific to this data / surprising; **Testability** = can the
data answer it credibly (sample size, confounders). H = high, M = medium, L = low.

| # | Question | Origin in the data | Why it matters | How to test | Decision | Novelty | Testability |
|---|----------|--------------------|----------------|-------------|----------|---------|-------------|
| 1 | Did the 25% across-the-board price increase in April 1997 reduce demand? | All 77 prices rose exactly 25% in the week of 1997-04-07; units/month did not fall afterwards. | Pricing is the most direct profit lever; if demand did not respond, there may be more pricing power. | Units, orders, active customers per month before vs after; same customers' quantities before vs after; by category and price tier. | H | H | M (all products changed at once and the business was growing, so no clean comparison group) |
| 2 | What is driving the 2.2x growth in Jan–Apr 1998: more customers, more frequent orders, or bigger orders? | Customers 65 → 80, orders/customer 1.89 → 3.20, avg order $1,555 → $1,650. | Tells sales where to focus: acquisition vs. order frequency vs. basket size. | Decompose revenue change into customers × frequency × order size; at constant prices; which customers contributed most. | H | M | H |
| 3 | Are short-deadline (14-day) orders handled any faster than normal ones? | 14-day orders late 18% vs 4% for 28-day orders, but median ship time is 7.5 vs 7 days. | Late deliveries hurt the customers who need speed most; an easy process fix. | Late rate and ship days by deadline window, within each shipper, employee and period; significance test (68 orders). | H | H | M (only 68 short-deadline orders, 12 late) |
| 4 | Do deeper discounts buy larger orders or more repeat business? | Discounts cost $88,666; quantity is ~25–28 units whether the rate is 5% or 25%. | If 25% buys the same as 5%, discount policy can be tightened without losing volume. | Quantity by rate within the same product; repeat orders after a discount; by employee. Correlation only, not causation. | H | M | M |
| 5 | Which country markets are growing or shrinking, and where should sales attention go? | Jan–Apr growth: USA +169%, Germany +167%, Brazil +286% vs Denmark −69%, Canada −24%, Mexico −44%. | Allocating sales effort across markets. | Country revenue trend at constant prices; how many customers drive each country's change. | H | L (this is the assignment's own example) | M (small countries have few orders) |
| 6 | Which valuable customers are slowing down or lapsing, compared with their own usual ordering rhythm? | 5 customers inactive 180+ days, incl. Mère Paillarde ($28,872); median gap between orders is 35 days. | A concrete retention call list. | Each customer's usual gap vs. days since last order; value at risk. | H | M | H |
| 7 | How dependent is revenue on a few customers and one product? | Top 3 customers = 25%; Côte de Blaye = 11.2% of revenue. | Concentration risk: losing one account or one supplier hurts a lot. | Pareto curves; trend in concentration over time; supplier of top products. | M | L | H |
| 8 | Can current inventory support current demand? | 38 of 69 active products hold < 1 month of stock at the 1998 sales rate. | Stock-outs lose sales in a fast-growing business. | Months of stock per product; revenue at risk. | H | M | L (date of the stock snapshot is unknown) |
| 9 | Is sales performance driven by the employee or by the customers they happen to serve? | Peacock is #1 overall but the only employee whose Jan–Apr revenue fell; customers are served by 5 different employees on average. | Account assignment and performance evaluation. | Employee revenue trend; overlap of customers; same customers' spend with different employees. | M | M | L (9 employees; easy to over-interpret) |
| 10 | Which orders are too small to cover their shipping cost? | Freight is 4.9% of order value at the median; one order's freight is 91% of its value; corr(freight, revenue) = 0.76. | Minimum order size or freight policy. | Freight / order value distribution; by country and shipper; how many orders exceed a threshold. | M | M | H |
